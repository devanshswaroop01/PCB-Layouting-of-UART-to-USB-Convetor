1. Introduction

This report documents the verification results obtained from the Electrical Rules Checker (ERC) and Design Rules Checker (DRC) performed on the USB-to-UART Converter PCB. These verification procedures are an essential part of the PCB design workflow and are used to identify potential electrical connectivity issues, schematic inconsistencies, and physical layout rule violations before fabrication.

The scope of this report is limited to the analysis of the generated ERC and DRC reports.

3. Electrical Rules Check (ERC)
Purpose

The Electrical Rules Check verifies the logical correctness of the schematic by analyzing:

Electrical connectivity
Pin compatibility
Net continuity
Symbol integrity
Unconnected pins and wires
Power connections

ERC is performed before PCB layout generation to ensure that the schematic accurately represents the intended electrical design.

4. ERC Summary

The generated ERC report indicates the following results.

Item	Result
Errors	0
Warnings	5
Interpretation

The absence of ERC errors indicates that no critical electrical connectivity or schematic rule violations were detected.

The reported warnings correspond to non-critical observations that do not prevent progression to PCB layout or fabrication.

5. ERC Warning Categories

The ERC report identified warnings related to:

Unconnected wire endpoints
Incomplete wire terminations
Non-critical schematic connectivity observations

These warnings are advisory in nature and should be reviewed during schematic refinement. They do not indicate functional electrical faults in the design.

6. Design Rules Check (DRC)
Purpose

The Design Rules Check verifies the physical PCB layout against predefined manufacturing and layout constraints.

The DRC evaluates:

Track clearances
Copper spacing
Pad connectivity
Silkscreen placement
Board edge clearance
Track continuity
Footprint placement
7. DRC Summary

The generated DRC report contains the following results.

Item	Result
Errors	0 Critical Errors
Warnings	29

The reported issues are primarily advisory and relate to layout presentation and manufacturability preferences rather than electrical correctness.

8. DRC Warning Categories

The warnings reported fall into the following categories.

8.1 Dangling Track Warnings

The report identifies a small number of track endpoints that are not connected to another conductive feature.

Typical causes include:

Short residual routing segments
Incomplete track cleanup
Unused routing remnants

These warnings should be reviewed and removed if they are unintended.

8.2 Silkscreen Clearance Warnings

Several silkscreen objects extend close to or beyond the PCB outline, particularly near connector J2.

These warnings indicate that portions of the printed legend may be clipped during board fabrication.

The warnings affect only the printed markings and do not impact the electrical operation of the PCB.

8.3 Silkscreen Text Size Warnings

The report indicates that some reference designator text has a height of 0.75 mm, while the configured minimum design rule is 0.80 mm.

This warning relates to readability and manufacturer-recommended silkscreen dimensions.

The text remains electrically irrelevant but may be more difficult to read on the fabricated PCB.

10. Significance of ERC and DRC

The verification process provides confidence that:

The schematic is electrically consistent.
The PCB layout complies with the defined design rules.
Potential cosmetic and manufacturability issues have been identified before fabrication.
The design can be further refined if desired without major architectural modifications.
11. Recommendations

Based on the verification reports, the following improvements may be considered:

Remove any unintended dangling track segments.
Adjust silkscreen objects to maintain clearance from the board outline.
Increase silkscreen reference text height to satisfy the configured minimum size, if improved readability is desired.
Perform a final ERC and DRC after incorporating any revisions.

These actions are intended to improve documentation quality and manufacturability rather than correct functional defects.

13. Conclusion

The ERC and DRC verification process was successfully completed for the USB-to-UART Converter PCB using KiCad 9.0.7. The ERC identified no electrical errors, confirming the logical integrity of the schematic, while the DRC reported no critical layout violations that would prevent PCB fabrication. The remaining observations consist of advisory warnings related to dangling tracks, silkscreen placement near the board outline, and silkscreen text dimensions. These findings do not indicate fundamental design flaws but provide opportunities for further refinement to enhance manufacturability and documentation quality.
